# Quickening Privacy Policy

**Effective date: July 11, 2026**

This is the privacy policy for the Quickening application, made by Tony Virelli.

The short version: **Quickening collects nothing.**

## Everything stays on your computer

Everything Quickening knows about your files — names, sizes, content hashes, scan history, removal history — lives in a local database on your own computer, under your Windows user profile at %LocalAppData%\Quickening. None of it is ever sent anywhere. There is no server. I cannot see your files, your file names, or anything about how you use the app.

Log files the app writes for troubleshooting stay on your machine too.

Files you remove with Quickening go to the Windows Recycle Bin — they are not permanently deleted, and they never leave your computer.

## No accounts, no ads, no tracking

Quickening has no sign-in, no advertising, no analytics, and no trackers. It does not read data from other apps and does not run in your browser.

## What about the internet?

The current version of Quickening makes no network connections at all.

A future version will check for updates by contacting GitHub (github.com, operated by Microsoft), where Quickening's releases are hosted. An update check sends only what any web request sends — your IP address and basic system details — and nothing about your files or your use of the app.

## Removing your data

Uninstall Quickening and delete the %LocalAppData%\Quickening folder. That is everything.

## The website and donations

The quickening.app website and donation pages are covered by the online privacy policy at quickening.app/privacy, which also describes the website's use of Google Analytics. Donations are processed by third parties (such as PayPal, Stripe, or Ko-fi) under their own privacy policies — I never see your payment details.

## Changes

If this policy changes, the updated version ships with the app and is posted at quickening.app. Any material change will be called out clearly before it takes effect.
